EXTEND TAKE - one continuous speech across chained windows (rendering
replays the previous window's last ~1 second at the head of the next window
and discards it; there is no cut, no hold, no settle anywhere)

The blocks you write are consecutive WINDOWS of ONE unbroken take of ONE
continuous speech. Write the whole speech first, then cut it into windows.

1. ONE SPEECH, CUT ONLY AT SENTENCE OR CLAUSE BOUNDARIES. Compose the entire
   take's speech as one continuous monologue (or one continuous exchange)
   sized to the TOTAL word budget you are given. Then split it into blocks
   ONLY where a sentence ends or a clause ends on a comma - never mid-clause,
   never on a dangling word ("...except mine. I" is wrong; end the block at
   "except mine." and start the next with "I sat here..."). Every block must
   read as complete speech on its own. Block k+1's line begins with the very
   next words after block k's line ended. Never repeat words already spoken.
   Never restart the thought.
2. NO AIRLOCK, NO SETTLE, NO SILENCE AT BOUNDARIES. Speech runs wall to wall
   through every boundary. Do not write pauses, holds, breaths or silences at
   the start or end of a block; the replay head is discarded automatically
   and you do not plan for it.
3. IDENTICAL EVERYTHING. Characters, wardrobe, room, light, camera position
   and framing are restated byte-identically in every block. One [Shot 1]
   per block, no camera cuts, no cut verbs, no <scenetrans>.
4. CONTINUOUS BUSINESS, NO EVENTS. Small physical business that flows across
   boundaries (turning a mug, a weight shift, a glance, hands moving with the
   words). Nothing enters, leaves, appears or changes at a boundary; the
   arrangement never changes for the whole take.
5. Every block SPEAKS, AND ROUGHLY EVENLY. Each block's line holds only that
   window's portion of the speech, at natural conversational pace, sized to
   the per-block budget. Even is a SOFT target: a few words more or less per
   block is fine and expected, because rule 1 wins - cut at the nearest
   sentence or clause boundary, not at an exact word count. What is NOT fine
   is a thin final block (a few words after a long silence) or any block
   under about 70% of the budget: do not front-load the speech
   (render-verified 2026-08-16).
